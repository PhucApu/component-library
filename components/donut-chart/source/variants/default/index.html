How the total divides

A ring answers one question well — how a total splits — and one question badly: which of two similar wedges is bigger. People read length accurately and angle poorly. So the numbers live in the legend rather than in the shape, and nobody is asked to estimate.

The hole is the reason it is a ring

A pie has nowhere to put the number the parts are parts of. The hole holds the total, and while a wedge is being read it holds that wedge instead — its name, its value and its share — so the answer appears where the eye already is rather than in a panel somewhere beside the pointer.

Two pixels of the card's own background separate the wedges. It is taken out of each wedge rather than drawn on top, so they still add up to the whole circle. A stroke around each one would be ink with the weight of data doing a spacer's job.
