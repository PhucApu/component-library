Six wedges, and the rest summed into one

Eleven rows go in and six wedges come out. Past six the small ones are slivers, their labels have nowhere to sit, and the shape stops answering the question it was drawn for. The note under the ring says how many were folded, and the table still has every one of them.

This is the opposite default from the line chart, on purpose. A ninth line is still a readable line; a ninth wedge is not a readable wedge.

Other is last, and it is not a category

The remainder keeps the last slot rather than being given a colour of its own, and it always sits at the end of the legend. An Other that turns up in the middle reads as a category somebody named, rather than as what is left.

One value column, and it says so

A ring divides one total. The table below has three value columns, and only the first is plotted — with the note saying so. Quietly drawing the first would let somebody believe the other two were in there somewhere.
