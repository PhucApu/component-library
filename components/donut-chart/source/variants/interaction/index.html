The answer appears where the eye already is

Point at a wedge and the hole becomes that wedge — its name, its value, its share. No panel floating beside the pointer: the middle of the ring is where a reader is already looking, and it was empty.

Tab to the ring and use ← → to walk it, Home and End for the ends, Escape to let go. The ring is one tab stop, not six: a chart that costs six presses to get past is a chart people learn to avoid.

Switching one off does not repaint the rest

Press a legend row. The colour belongs to the row's position in the table, not to its rank among whatever is currently shown — so hiding Engineering leaves Sales orange rather than promoting it to blue. The shares recalculate against what is left, because that is what a part-to-whole chart is for, and the total in the hole follows.

A hidden row is struck through as well as faded, and the last visible wedge will not switch off — an empty ring under a full legend reads as a fault rather than as a choice.
