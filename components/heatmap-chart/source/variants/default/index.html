Colour carrying size rather than identity

The other charts here use colour to say which — which series, which source. This one uses it to say how much, and the rules are almost the opposite: one hue instead of eight, light to dark instead of a fixed order, and a scale legend that can never be dropped. A colour meaning "a lot" says nothing until the reader is told how much a lot is.

Five steps, and that is a limit

Past about seven classes the neighbouring steps blur and the reader is back to consulting the legend for every cell — which is the thing a colour scale exists to avoid. Five are distinguishable and, more to the point, memorable. Measured, the closest neighbouring pair is 1.36 on the light surface and 1.42 on the dark one.

No numbers in the squares

A heatmap with a figure in every cell is a table. Printing one would also mean choosing the ink per step, since the same grey cannot sit on both ends of the scale. The value is in the read-out, in the cell's name, and in the table below — three ways to get it without making the grid unreadable.
