Take hold of it and pull

A touch screen swipes on its own; a mouse cannot, so the pointer drag is built. Snapping is switched off for as long as the drag lasts — with it on, every pixel is pulled straight back to the nearest snap point and the strip refuses to move.

Two things commit a drag, and either is enough: travelling a fifth of a picture, or still moving quickly when you let go. Distance alone would throw away the short flick that is how most people move a carousel.

  • Drag me, or flick me

  • A short quick flick is enough

  • A slow drag has to go further

  • Let go short and it falls back

A picture that is also a link

Dragging across a link must not follow it when you let go. The press is watched, and once it has travelled far enough to be a drag the click that follows is stopped before it reaches the link.

Dragging turned off

no-drag leaves the arrows, the dots, the keyboard and the browser's own swiping alone. Only the pointer drag goes.
