Held still

paused stops the drift and leaves everything else: the arrow keys still turn the ring, dragging still works, and the picture facing you stays singled out — which is why this ring starts with its first picture out in front. It is also what pause() and resume() set, so a page that stops the ring for its own reasons and the attribute never disagree, and resume() is what starts the drift again after a reader has turned the ring by hand.

A picture that never arrived

The second place on this ring points at a file that is not there. The ring keeps its shape and the place keeps its turn, because a gap that closed up would move every other picture for a reason the reader cannot see. What shows instead says so in words rather than leaving an empty frame.

One picture

One picture has no ring to be on: the radius is zero, so it stands in the middle and stays there, and the arrows are not drawn at all because there is nowhere to step to. Drifting a single picture would turn it away from the reader for half of every turn and show them nothing.

Under prefers-reduced-motion every ring on this page behaves like this one: no drift, no coasting after a throw, and the arrow keys arrive without travelling.
