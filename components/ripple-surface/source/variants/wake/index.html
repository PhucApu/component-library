The wake on its own

no-drop leaves only the trail. The pointer is a prow: water is pushed aside into two sides that meet in a point at the pointer itself and open out behind it, wider the further back they go, thinning and fading as they widen.

Each side is three strands rather than one line, at slightly different distances and out of step with one another. Two waves of different lengths run along each strand, and every point of the trail keeps a small wobble of its own from the moment it was made. One clean stroke a side is a diagram of a wake; a band of crests that never quite repeats is water.

The angle they open at is fixed, the way a real wake holds its angle however fast the hull is going. Speed decides how strongly the wake draws, not what shape it is — a shape that swelled and collapsed with every change of pace would read as the V breathing rather than as water being pushed out of the way.

Turn a corner and the wake follows the path you actually took rather than swinging round to your new heading.

Sampled coarsely, and finely

spacing is how often the path is sampled, in pixels travelled — never once per pointer event, because event rates differ by browser and by hardware and the same gesture would look different on different machines.

The same surface at spacing="40" and spacing="6". Coarse sampling shows the corners of the path it was built from; fine sampling reads as one curve and costs more to draw.
