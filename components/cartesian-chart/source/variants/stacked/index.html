Part to whole, over time

Stacking answers two questions at once: how big the total is, and how it splits. It answers the first well and the second only for the segment sitting on the baseline — every band above it starts somewhere different each month, and lengths that do not share a start are hard to compare. Grouped columns are the better answer when the split is the question.

The gap is the surface, not a border

Two pixels of the card's own background separate every segment from the one above it, and every column from its neighbour. It is taken out of the mark rather than drawn on top, so the segments still add up to their own total. A stroke around each band would be ink with the weight of data doing a spacer's job.

A missing week is missing

A blank cell adds nothing to the stack and draws no band. Counting it as zero would keep the total the same height while a segment quietly disappeared, which is the same lie a line chart tells when it draws straight through a gap.
