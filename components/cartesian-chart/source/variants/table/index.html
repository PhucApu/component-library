The markup is the data

The whole contract is an ordinary <table>: first column the categories, every column after it a series named by its heading. Nothing is configured in JavaScript and nothing is fetched.

Press Show the table. It is not a copy generated beside the chart — it is what the chart was drawn from, so the two cannot drift apart. Turn scripting off and it is all that is left, which for a page of numbers is most of what anyone came for.

Written for a person, plotted as a number

The cells say 18.4 TB and the chart plots 18.4. Where the text cannot be parsed with confidence — a decimal comma, an accounting figure in brackets, a word — data-value settles it and the cell goes on reading however it was written. The read-out shows the author's own wording rather than a reformatted version of the same number.

An empty cell is missing, not zero

The Americas figure for Q3 was never collected, so the cell is empty and the area breaks across it. Drawing it as zero would put a confident dip in a quarter nobody measured — the one mistake in this component that would be invisible in the chart and obvious in the table.

Why it can be hidden but never removed

Collapsed, the table is hidden from the eye and not from the page: a screen reader still has every number while the drawing beside it stays out of the way. Three of the eight light-theme series colours sit below the contrast rule on white by design, and that is only allowed where the values are readable without colour as well. The table is what makes that true, so there is no attribute to take it away.
