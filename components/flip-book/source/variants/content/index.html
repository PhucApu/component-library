Pages are content, not pictures

A page is whatever the author wrote. These carry headings, text and a link, and they are the same elements that were in the list before the book was made — moved into the leaves rather than copied, so anything already bound to them still works. Try the link on page 3, and try dragging across it: a drag never follows a link.

Five pages make three leaves, and the back of the last one has nothing on it. A book with an odd page count ends on a blank; dropping the leaf instead would take the page in front of it with it.

  • On still water

    A surface at rest is the absence of an animation rather than a slow one. What follows is a short argument for leaving things alone until they are asked for.

  • One number

    Describe a whole state with one number and the states cannot disagree with one another. A book knows how many leaves have been turned; everything else follows from that.

  • A page that links out

    This page carries a link. Press it and it responds; drag across it and the book turns instead.

  • Two sides

    A sheet of paper has a front and a back, so a leaf carries two pages. That is the whole reason a turn leaves something behind on the left.

  • The last page

    Five pages, three leaves, and the back of this one is blank — as it would be in print.
