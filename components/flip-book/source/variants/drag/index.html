Two things commit a turn

Take the page past half way and it falls over on its own. Let go short of that and it falls back. Either way the leaf carries on from the angle your hand left it at, which is why the turn is driven frame by frame rather than handed to a transition: a transition only knows how to go from one value to another, not how to pick up an arc already in progress.

Distance is not the only way to commit one. A short flick counts too — that is how most people turn a page — so still moving quickly when you let go is enough.

Pull the other way to put a page back. Which leaf is taken depends on the direction your hand went, not on where it landed.

A book that will not be dragged

no-drag leaves the arrows and the keyboard exactly as they are and takes only the gesture away, for a page where sideways dragging already means something else.
