A ratio against a limit is a meter, not a pie

Two slices of a circle is the hardest possible way to read one percentage. A bar with a ceiling says the same thing in a shape people already read left to right, and it leaves room for the actual numbers beside it.

The ceiling is markup, not an attribute: <p class="stat-tile__limit" data-value="1024">of 1 TB</p>. Same rule as everywhere else here — the numbers live in the page, and the wording stays whatever the author wrote.

The track is the fill's own hue

The unfilled part is the fill mixed into the surface, so the whole bar reads as one measurement rather than as two different things meeting. Measured fill against track: 3.80 light and 4.14 dark while it is comfortable, 4.03 and 3.31 once it is critical — all clear of the 3:1 a boundary has to reach.

Except one, and it is said out loud instead

Amber on a light surface reaches only 1.70 against its own track, and no lighter step of amber does any better — the colour is simply too pale to carry a boundary there. So the meter never relies on it: the words nearing the limit and at the limit appear beside the numbers in every state that has one. Press 79% and read it with the colour ignored.
