The tapering shape is the problem

The classic funnel encodes the value as a width but hands the eye an area to read — and the area of a trapezoid is not proportional to the number it stands for. A stage at half the count comes out looking far worse than half, every time, in the same direction. Bars from a shared left edge put the value in a length, which is the one visual channel people compare accurately.

One colour, because position already says the order

Shading each stage a different depth spends the only free channel restating something the layout already shows. Colour is better spent on the loss.

Six steps, and the ramp stops there

Ordinal colour is a third problem again. A categorical palette needs neighbours to stay apart under colour blindness; a sequential ramp may let its palest step recede toward the surface, because "near zero" is allowed to look like near nothing. These are discrete marks that all have to be seen, so every step clears 2:1 against the surface — measured at 2.11 light and 2.15 dark, with the closest neighbouring pair at 1.42 and 1.35.

Past six there is no seventh step that stays apart from the sixth. Nothing is discarded when a funnel is longer — summing two stages of a funnel would be meaningless, unlike summing two slices of a donut — so the ramp falls back to one colour and says so underneath.
